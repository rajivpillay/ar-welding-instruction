Unit objective

Given a set of CTE Welding and Materials Joining pathway standards, analyze which can be supported by AR simulator practice and which require live instruction, completing every row of the alignment table.

What you’ll do

Complete the Alignment Table. For each of seven California welding standards, decide whether AR simulator practice can support it fully, partly, or not at all, and say why. Then flag any safety requirement that has to be taught at the live arc.

Choose how you’ll do it

Use the seven California standards in the table. If you teach outside California, or want to rate standards from your own program as well, add up to two of your own at the end of the table. The rubric applies to every row you fill in.

Before you start

Read the rubric first so you know what a complete table looks like. When you finish, you’ll score your own work with it.

Worked example: Unit 3 Task

This example uses different standards from the ones in your table. Use it to see what complete rows look like, then close it and fill in your own.

C3.1

Use welding tools such as OFW, SMAW, GMAW, FCAW, GTAW, forge, and furnace and the equipment and assembly processes appropriate to the design criteria of a specific product to result in a finished part or product that meets the standards of the AWS or similar industry welding standards.

  • Rating: Partially supported
  • The part the simulator covers: Welding technique for GMAW, SMAW, GTAW, and FCAW, in all four positions.
  • Reason: The standard asks for a finished part, assembled to a product’s design criteria and meeting an AWS standard. The simulator builds the technique; the assembly and the finished part are live work.
  • Safety taught live: Yes. Hazards: Arc radiation; fumes and gases; electrical shock; fire and burns; noise; cables and tripping

C1.2

Interpret scaled welding blueprints; gather design and materials information; perform calculations; and use the detail to plan, lay out, and produce parts or finished products.

  • Rating: Not supported
  • The part the simulator covers: —
  • Reason: The standard asks students to read prints, calculate, lay out, and produce parts. Simulator exercises come already set up, so none of that is practiced.
  • Safety taught live: Yes. Hazards: Fire and burns; arc radiation; fumes and gases

C2.2

Use standard and new emerging welding tools and equipment, such as oxygen fuel cutting (OFC), plasma arc cutting (PAC), and carbon arc cutting (CAC) to cut materials for the purpose of completing a finished product that meets the standards of the AWS or a similar industry standard.

  • Rating: Not supported
  • The part the simulator covers: —
  • Reason: The standard is about cutting. Simulators are used for joining, not cutting. Cutting is a foundational shop skill practiced alongside simulator welding.
  • Safety taught live: Yes. Hazards: Arc radiation; fumes and gases; fire and burns; noise; electrical shock

How it scores

Rubric: The rating, 2. Each rating rests on what simulator practice exercises: technique for C3.1, nothing for C1.2 and C2.2.

Rubric: The reason, 2. Each reason starts from what the standard asks the student to do or produce, and the partial rating names the part. “Partially supported, because the simulator covers welding” would score 1: it names no part and does not say what is left for live work.

Rubric: The safety flag, 2. Every standard that involves live work is flagged, with the hazards named. A “Yes” with no hazards chosen would score 1.

Standards quoted from California Department of Education (2017), MPD 14–15.
