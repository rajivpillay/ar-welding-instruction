Unit objective

Using your completed alignment table, develop an AR-to-live-welding integration plan for one course in one upcoming term, completing every section of the plan template.

What you’ll do

Build an AR-to-live-welding Integration Plan for one course you will teach in an upcoming term. Your Unit 3 Alignment Table fills in the first part. You complete the three sections: organize tools, prepare resources, and adapt the lab setting.

Choose how you’ll do it

Pick the course the plan is for. When you finish, download it as a written plan, a table for a spreadsheet, or a week-by-week calendar.

Before you start

Read the rubric first so you know what a complete plan looks like. When you finish, you’ll score your own work with it.

Worked example: Unit 4 Task

A made-up plan for a different course, built from the Unit 3 worked example (C3.1, C1.2, C2.2). Use it to see what a complete plan looks like, then close it and write your own.

Plan basics

Course: Welding Technology 1

Term: Spring, 18 weeks

Students and stations: 24 students, 4 simulator stations

From Unit 3

Must teach live: C3.1, the finished assembly (arc radiation, fumes and gases, electrical shock, fire and burns, noise, cables and tripping). C1.2, producing parts from prints (fire and burns, arc radiation, fumes and gases). C2.2, cutting (arc radiation, fumes and gases, fire and burns, noise, electrical shock).

What the simulator handles: C3.1, partially: welding technique for GMAW, SMAW, GTAW, and FCAW in all four positions.

1. Organize tools

Schedule: Every student uses the simulator twice a week for 20 minutes, in weeks 1 to 10, in groups of four that rotate through the stations.

Sequence: GMAW fillet welds flat, then horizontal, vertical, and overhead; then FCAW; then SMAW; groove welds after fillets in each process.

Report log: Every Friday I review each student’s attempt record and write one line per student: the exercise, the overall scores, and any parameter in red.

Rubric: Consistency, 2. Every learner has regular time, how often and how long, and the log is kept and reviewed weekly. “Students use the simulator when booths are full” would score 1.

2. Prepare resources

Demonstrations: A short demonstration on the simulator and at a booth each time the class moves to a new position (weeks 2, 3, and 4) and a new process (weeks 5 and 7).

Live instruction: C2.2: cutting demonstrations and practice in the shop in weeks 3 to 6, alongside simulator welding, with cutting safety taught first. C1.2: print reading and layout in weeks 6 to 9, then producing a part from a print. C3.1: a final assembly in weeks 14 to 18.

Before the first live arc: PPE check, a condition check of the machine and booth, and an inventory of pliers, wire brushes, and chipping hammers.

Rubric: Live instruction, 2. A demonstration at each switch, every must-teach-live standard placed in the term with its safety, and a pre-live routine. A plan that leaves out C2.2 or its safety would score 1.

3. Adapt the lab setting

Readiness rule: A student is ready to move from simulator to booth when their last 3 to 5 sessions on the exercise show consistent overall scores at or above target, with no parameter in red across those sessions.

Re-orientation phase: The first three live sessions are short, with a break every 15 minutes. Students pair up after each session to share what felt different. I check in with each student at the end of their first session.

Rotation: Once students start live welding, half the class is at the booths while the other half uses the simulators or cuts and preps coupons, switching at mid-period.

Rubric: The transition, 2. The rule uses a pattern across sessions, and the re-orientation plan names the sessions, the breaks, and support from classmates. “Ready after 10 hours on the simulator” would score 1.
