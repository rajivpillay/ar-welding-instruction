2. Work angle

Sample wording: the side-to-side tilt of the gun relative to the joint.

This attempt averaged 38° and scored 95. The numbers at the ends of the graph read 30 and 60.

3. Travel angle

Sample wording: the forward or backward tilt of the gun along the direction of travel, push or drag.

This attempt averaged 29° and scored 87, the only parameter highlighted red.

4. Contact-tip-to-work distance (CTWD)

Sample wording: the distance from the contact tip to the work.

Averaged 0.75 in. and scored 94. The line touches the top of the graph near the end of the weld.

5. Travel speed

Sample wording: how fast the gun moves along the joint.

Averaged 9.7 in/min and scored 98.

6. Aim

Sample wording: where the wire points relative to the center of the joint.

Averaged 0.07 in. and scored 97.

7. Voltage and wire feed speed

Machine settings, checked against the exercise.

18.3 V and 293 IPM. Both scored 100.

8. Trace line

Each line shows one reading across the length of the weld, left to right.

Red sections mark where the system flagged the reading. Travel angle averages 29°, but the line reaches the top of the graph twice. The average hides that.

9. Parameter scores

Each parameter gets its own score.

Travel angle, 87, is highlighted red. The other four scored 94 to 98. A passing attempt can still have one parameter that did not pass.

Sample 5 · Write, then compare

Red at the booth

Would serve: Unit 2, Knowledge Check, outcome 3

During a 2F fillet exercise, a student’s travel speed guide keeps turning red. The report afterward shows travel speed averaging 13.6 in/min against a target of 8.0.

Model cues

Sample wording. Rajiv writes the final cues.

Strong: “Slow your hand down. Let the puddle wet into both toes before you move forward.”
It names one change the student can make right now.

Strong: “Watch the puddle, not the guide. Move only as fast as it fills the joint.”
It gives the student something to look at that carries over to live welding.

Weak: “Your travel speed was 13.6 inches per minute and the target is 8.”
It reads the number aloud. The student cannot act on inches per minute at the booth.
