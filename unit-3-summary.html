Unit objective

Given a set of CTE Welding and Materials Joining pathway standards, analyze which can be supported by AR simulator practice and which require live instruction, completing every row of the alignment table.

The big idea

A standard says what a student must do and produce. Simulator practice builds the movements. Preparation, real metal, cutting, testing, and a finished product that meets an industry code come from live work. Rating each standard honestly, and naming the part when support is partial, shows you where simulator time fits and what has to stay in the booth. And every standard that involves live work carries safety requirements the simulator does not teach.

What to carry forward

  • Read the verb, then the product. “Meets the standards of the AWS” means proficiency shown on real work, measured against a code such as AWS D1.1/D1.1M:2025.
  • Simulators are built for joining: four processes, four positions, fillet and groove joints. Joint preparation, tacking, grinding, cutting, heat, spatter, noise, and smoke are live.
  • Rate what practice exercises, not the score or the vendor’s list. A partial rating names the part.
  • Safety comes from Anchor Standard 6.0 and ANSI Z49.1. The simulator can start the helmet habit. Everything else is taught at the live arc, beginning with a PPE and equipment check.
  • Expect a re-orientation phase when students move to live welding.

Coming up in Unit 4

Unit 3 sorted your standards into what simulator practice can support and what has to be taught live. Unit 4 turns that table into a plan: one course, one term, with simulator practice and live welding in sequence.
