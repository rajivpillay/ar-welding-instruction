About this course

Sim to Steel is a self-paced professional development course for Career and Technical Education welding instructors who are preparing to bring augmented reality simulation into an existing welding program. No prior AR experience is needed. You work through four units at your own pace and finish with an integration plan for one of your courses. See the Introduction for why the course exists and who it is for.

Course objectives

By the end of the course, you will be able to:

  1. Using the provided AR System Evaluation, evaluate an AR welding training system for fit with your existing welding theory and lab practices, completing every section.
  2. Given a sample AR simulator performance report, interpret the feedback data and provide constructive input to the welding student, identifying the indicator outside the acceptable range and recommending one specific technique modification.
  3. Given a set of CTE Welding and Materials Joining pathway standards, analyze which can be supported by AR simulator practice and which require live instruction, completing every row of the alignment table.
  4. Using your completed alignment table, develop an AR-to-live-welding integration plan for one course in one upcoming term, completing every section of the plan template.

Units

Each unit builds toward your integration plan. Work through them in order: Unit 4 starts from the Alignment Table you complete in Unit 3.

Unit 1: Orientation and Goal Setting

Where AR fits in a welding program.

  • Objective: Using the provided AR System Evaluation, evaluate an AR welding training system for fit with your existing welding theory and lab practices, completing every section.
  • Unit Task: Use the AR System Evaluation to evaluate one AR welding training system for your program, ending with your recommendation.
  • Estimated time: about 60 minutes, not counting breaks.

Unit 2: The AR Session and Its Feedback

What a session produces, and how to read it back to a student.

  • Objective: Given a sample AR simulator performance report, interpret the feedback data and provide constructive input to the welding student, identifying the indicator outside the acceptable range and recommending one specific technique modification.
  • Unit Task: Read a sample simulator report, name the indicator outside the acceptable range, and write the one technique change you would recommend.
  • Estimated time: about 75 minutes, not counting breaks.

Unit 3: Aligning AR with Curriculum and Safety

Connecting simulator evidence to curriculum standards, objectives, and lab safety.

  • Objective: Given a set of CTE Welding and Materials Joining pathway standards, analyze which can be supported by AR simulator practice and which require live instruction, completing every row of the alignment table.
  • Unit Task: Complete the Alignment Table for seven California welding standards, and flag the safety requirements that must be taught live.
  • Estimated time: about 75 minutes, not counting breaks.

Unit 4: Building an Integration Plan

Moving students from simulated practice to live welding.

  • Objective: Using your completed alignment table, develop an AR-to-live-welding integration plan for one course in one upcoming term, completing every section of the plan template.
  • Unit Task: Build an Integration Plan for one course in one upcoming term, starting from your Alignment Table.
  • Estimated time: about 90 minutes, not counting breaks.

How each unit works

Every unit has the same six parts. The sidebar on each unit page shows where you are.

  1. Overview: what the unit covers, its objective, and what you will be able to do.
  2. Pre-Quiz: five questions before the lessons begin. It isn’t graded. You see your score, but not the answers.
  3. Knowledge Building: the instruction itself, with short “Try it” activities that give feedback as you go.
  4. Knowledge Check: ten questions with feedback. The first five are like the Pre-Quiz, so you can see what changed. If you get 8 or more right, you’re ready for the Unit Task. If not, the results point you to the topics to review, and you can try again or continue.
  5. Unit Task: where you show you can meet the unit objective.
  6. Summary: what to carry into the next unit.

Use Mark complete at the bottom of each part to keep track of where you are.

Unit Task rubrics

Each Unit Task has a rubric with three criteria, each scored 2 (Complete), 1 (Partial), or 0 (Not yet), for 6 points in all. The rubric is at the top of each Unit Task page. Read it before you start, so you know what complete work looks like.

  • Each task has a worked example on a different case, scored against the same rubric.
  • When you finish, you score your own work with the rubric. A 2 in every row meets the unit objective.
  • There is nothing to submit. Download a copy of your work to keep.

Planning Tools

The Planning Tools page keeps the course’s tools in one place, so you can return to them after the course.

  • AR System Evaluation: used in the Unit 1 Task.
  • Alignment Table: used in the Unit 3 Task.
  • AR-to-Live-Welding Integration Plan: used in the Unit 4 Task; starts from your Alignment Table.
  • Simulator Feedback Reference: what a simulator reports and where to find it on the screen.

Glossary

Select the information icon after a term on any unit page for a short definition. The Glossary lists every term, tagged by where it comes from: general AR terms, Miller terms, CTE standards terms, course terms, and welding terms.

Your work stays in your browser

The course has no accounts and no sign-in. Your answers, scores, and progress save in the browser you use, on that device only, and are not sent anywhere. Clearing your browser’s site data erases them, and they don’t follow you to another device. Download a copy of each Unit Task when you finish.

Accessibility

Every page works with a keyboard, every image has a text description, and videos come with a written transcript or description. Use the Dark mode button in the header to switch themes.
