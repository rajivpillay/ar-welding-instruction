Worked example: Unit 1 Task

This example evaluates a made-up system, not the one you are evaluating. Use it to see what a complete task looks like, then close it and write your own.

About this evaluation

System: Example System A (made up for this example).
What I used: vendor materials — the brochure, the website, and a recorded demo.

My goals

Operational: Move beginning students’ first two weeks of practice out of the booths and into the classroom next door, so the booths open up for advanced students.

Instructional: Beginning students hold a steady travel speed on a 2F fillet on the simulator before their first live weld.

Rubric: Your two goals, 2. Each goal names something you could check at the end of a term: where the first two weeks of practice happened, and whether students held a steady speed before their first live weld. A goal like “Use AR to improve our program” scores 1, because there is nothing to check.

Part 1: Checklist (two of the eight areas)

Areas 3 and 6. The other six areas are answered the same way.
QuestionAnswer
3. Does it score each part of technique: angles, distance, speed, and aim?Yes
3. Does it show the acceptable range for each exercise?Not sure
3. Does it save a record of every attempt?Yes
6. Does the vendor offer evidence that skills carry over to live welding?Yes
6. Does that evidence come from a study, rather than testimonials?No

Notes on feedback and reports: The brochure shows scores but no ranges. Vendor question: Where does an instructor or student see the acceptable range for each exercise?

Notes on carry-over: The only evidence is two instructor quotes on the website. Vendor question: Has any study compared students who trained on this system with students who didn’t?

Rubric: The checklist, 2. Every item is answered, and the “Not sure” became a question for the vendor. The “No” on evidence did too.

Part 2: Short report

  1. Operational goal: Likely met. It runs on a tabletop stand from a wall outlet, with no arc or fumes, so it can be set up in the classroom.
  2. Instructional goal: Partly met. It scores travel speed on every attempt, which my goal needs. I can’t tell yet whether it shows the acceptable range, so I don’t know how a student would know what “steady” means.
  3. Biggest strength: Every attempt is saved and scored, so I can see whether a student improves over a week, not just on one weld.
  4. Biggest limitation: No evidence beyond testimonials that practice on it carries over to live welding.

Recommendation

Pilot it first. It fits the classroom and scores what my instructional goal needs, but I want answers on ranges and carry-over before we commit.

Rubric: Report and recommendation, 2. The report answers both goals, names one strength and one limitation, and the recommendation follows from the two open questions in the checklist.
